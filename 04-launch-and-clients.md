# Launch and Clients  
*(Contracts, Disclosures, and AI-Related Risks)*

## Context
The launch phase marks the point where a software product or service **interacts with external users or paying clients**.

At this stage:
- commitments are formalised
- representations are made to clients
- data responsibilities become externally visible
- AI features move from experimentation to real-world use

Decisions taken here shape **legal exposure, client trust, and long-term relationships**.

---

## Common Risk Areas

### 1. Misalignment Between Product Capabilities and Contractual Promises

**What typically happens**  
Sales or marketing descriptions promise outcomes that the product or AI system cannot reliably deliver.

**Why it happens**  
- Pressure to close deals  
- Limited coordination between sales, legal, and technical teams  
- Overconfidence in AI capabilities  

**Risk**  
Contracts may create obligations beyond the system’s actual performance.

**Impact**  
- Client disputes  
- Refund or penalty claims  
- Loss of credibility  

**Preventive thinking**  
Ensure contracts and marketing language accurately reflect system capabilities and limitations.

---

### 2. Lack of Clear Disclosure of AI Usage

**What typically happens**  
AI features are embedded into products without clearly informing clients or users.

**Why it happens**  
- AI viewed as an internal implementation detail  
- Fear that disclosure may raise concerns  

**Risk**  
Clients may be unaware that outputs are AI-generated or probabilistic in nature.

**Impact**  
- Misuse or over-reliance on outputs  
- Complaints when results are inconsistent  
- Trust erosion  

**Preventive thinking**  
Disclose AI usage at an appropriate level and explain limitations in plain language.

---

### 3. Unclear Responsibility for AI Outputs

**What typically happens**  
It is unclear whether responsibility lies with:
- the software provider  
- the client using the system  
- the AI tool or model provider  

**Why it happens**  
- Complex AI supply chains  
- Generic contract templates  

**Risk**  
Ambiguity over liability when AI-generated outputs cause harm or loss.

**Impact**  
- Legal disputes  
- Strained client relationships  

**Preventive thinking**  
Define responsibility boundaries clearly in contracts and usage terms.

---

### 4. Data Ownership and Intellectual Property in AI Systems

**What typically happens**  
Contracts do not clearly address ownership of:
- training data  
- fine-tuned models  
- AI-generated outputs  

**Why it happens**  
- Traditional IP clauses reused without adaptation  
- Limited understanding of AI workflows  

**Risk**  
Disputes over ownership and usage rights.

**Impact**  
- Client conflicts  
- Restrictions on future development  
- Loss of strategic assets  

**Preventive thinking**  
Clarify ownership and usage rights related to data and AI outputs upfront.

---

### 5. Client Data Used for AI Training or Improvement

**What typically happens**  
Client data is reused to improve models or features without explicit agreement.

**Why it happens**  
- Desire to improve accuracy  
- Assumption of implied permission  

**Risk**  
Processing data beyond client expectations or contractual scope.

**Impact**  
- Breach of trust  
- Contractual or regulatory exposure  

**Preventive thinking**  
Explicitly state whether and how client data may be used for training or improvement.

---

### 6. Automated Decision-Making Affecting End Users

**What typically happens**  
AI systems influence decisions such as recommendations, rankings, or approvals.

**Why it happens**  
- Efficiency gains  
- Reduced human involvement  

**Risk**  
Users may be affected by decisions without understanding how they are made.

**Impact**  
- User dissatisfaction  
- Reputational harm  
- Increased scrutiny  

**Preventive thinking**  
Include human oversight where appropriate and explain decision logic at a functional level.

---

### 7. Limitation of Liability in AI-Driven Systems

**What typically happens**  
Standard limitation clauses are applied without considering AI-specific risks.

**Why it happens**  
- Use of generic contract templates  
- Underestimation of AI uncertainty  

**Risk**  
Mismatch between risk exposure and contractual protection.

**Impact**  
- Unexpected liability  
- Commercial losses  

**Preventive thinking**  
Align liability clauses with realistic AI risk scenarios.

---

### 8. Updates, Model Changes, and Client Expectations

**What typically happens**  
AI models evolve over time, changing outputs after launch.

**Why it happens**  
- Continuous learning  
- Model retraining  

**Risk**  
Clients may experience changed behaviour without warning.

**Impact**  
- Confusion  
- Loss of confidence  

**Preventive thinking**  
Communicate updates clearly and manage expectations around AI variability.

---

## Why this stage is critical
Launch is the point where **internal assumptions meet external accountability**.

AI-related risks that remain abstract during development become **commercial and reputational risks** once clients are involved.

---

## Key takeaway
AI systems require **clear communication, realistic commitments, and defined responsibility**.

Most disputes arise not from AI failure, but from **unclear expectations and documentation**.

---

## Note
This section focuses on **risk awareness and contractual thinking**, not legal drafting or advice.

The objective is to help software teams and service providers launch AI-enabled products more responsibly and transparently.
